About

I am a Ph.D. student in Computer Engineering at Northeastern University, working in the Spatial Intelligence Research Group (SINRG) with Prof. Mallesham Dasari. I research wireless sensing and communication for AR/VR and smart glasses and low-power wearable sensing: building systems that help everyday devices understand and interact with the physical world around them.

I enjoy working across the full stack, from sensor and hardware prototyping to signal processing, on-device machine learning, and wireless connectivity. I am especially drawn to problems where careful systems design can make intelligent sensing practical on small, low-power devices, and to applications that have real impact on people.

Before my Ph.D., I earned a B.S. in Electrical & Computer Engineering from Norwich University (minors in Mathematics and Computer Science, GPA 3.97), where I was named Student Engineer of the Year and served as President of Eta Kappa Nu.

I am always open to new research collaborations. If our interests overlap, feel free to reach out at khalaf.m@northeastern.edu.

Research

Sensing the world with smaller, smarter devices.

Low-power wearable sensing+

Camera-free sensing modalities that fit a wearable's power envelope. CapGaze tracks gaze with periocular capacitive electrodes and a reflective photosensor, running real-time estimation on an embedded microcontroller.

Wireless performance of smart glasses+

Measuring how multimodal apps on commercial XR and AI glasses use the network, including end-to-end latency, throughput, and rail-level power over Bluetooth/BLE, Wi-Fi Direct, and 5G, to inform system and protocol design.

Open, connected smart-glasses hardware+

Drakon is an open smart-glasses prototype with native LTE-M cellular connectivity, so glasses can stay connected without a tethered phone.

Immersive systems+

Collaborations across the lab on volumetric video streaming under real-world network traces and on immersive VR training evaluated through user studies.

Publications

Wearable systems, sensing, and applied machine learning.
